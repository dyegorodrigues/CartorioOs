# GX Cartório OS — Bank DNA & Temporal Drift Protocol

Status: v0.1 research protocol

## Objective
Model how each examining institution turns legal knowledge into assessment, without treating a bank as a timeless personality and without confusing legal coverage with style inference.

## Unit of analysis
A bank profile is estimated by **bank + time window + legal complexity + exam family + phase**.

Examples:
- FGV / ENAC / 2025–2026 / objective / L4;
- FGV / cartório estadual / 2023–2026 / written-practical / L4;
- FGV / ENAM-magistracy / recent / objective / L4;
- Cebraspe / cartório / recent / objective / L4;
- Vunesp / TJSP cartório / recent / objective / L4.

Do not pool all FGV exams since inception into one style estimate.

## Dimensions to measure
Per period and corpus:
- stem length and information density;
- proportion of concrete cases vs abstract propositions;
- literal-law reliance;
- jurisprudence reliance;
- CNJ/operational regulation reliance;
- recency of legal sources;
- interdisciplinarity;
- cognitive demand: recognition, application, discrimination, integration;
- distractor mechanisms;
- use of negative commands;
- numerical/deadline/requisite density;
- source-family distribution;
- proportion of controversial or annulled items;
- phase-specific behavior;
- semantic novelty relative to previous exams.

## Temporal drift
Compare adjacent windows rather than only aggregate history.

For each dimension record:
- level in older window;
- level in recent window;
- direction and effect magnitude;
- sample size;
- confidence;
- plausible external cause (new edital, law, CNJ rule, exam redesign) when evidenced.

A trend is not promoted merely because two recent questions look similar.

## Predictive use
Prediction means prioritization under uncertainty, not fortune telling.

Signals can include:
- repeated microtopics;
- persistent source families;
- newly enacted high-impact norms;
- topics explicitly added or expanded in the newest matrix;
- concepts that bridge highly weighted domains;
- stable distractor mechanisms;
- official correction patterns in written/practical exams.

Every predicted priority must retain a confidence label and evidence trail.

## AI-authorship hypothesis
Do **not** infer that a question was generated by AI merely from wording or style. Unless an organizer publicly documents AI use or there is other reliable evidence, authorship technology remains unknown.

What GX may legitimately track is **observable style drift**: wording regularity, semantic structure, template similarity, novelty, source mix, distractor construction and error/annulment patterns. These can change for many reasons, including examiner composition, editorial processes or tooling. Causal attribution to AI requires evidence.

## Source reconstruction
For each high-value question, distinguish:
1. official question and key/mirror;
2. authoritative legal basis sufficient to answer it;
3. likely source family;
4. optional qualified commentary;
5. current-law status.

Never invent a specific book/article as the examiner's source without evidence.

## Cross-bank comparison
Maintain separate profiles. Use another bank for:
- domain coverage;
- transfer;
- rare microtopics;
- alternative distractors;
- written/practical/oral exemplars.

Do not let another bank silently redefine the target bank's style weights.

## Minimum evidence gates
- descriptive claim: can be made on a complete exam or a clearly labeled partial sample;
- time-window comparison: requires enough comparable items in both windows and matrix normalization;
- predictive claim: requires multi-edition evidence or strong external signal and must be confidence-labeled;
- causal claim about why a bank changed: requires external evidence, not stylistic speculation.
