# GX Cartório OS — Baseline Diagnostic Protocol

Atualizado em 09/09/2026.

## Goal
Estimate the candidate's real re-entry state without confusing `never exposed to Notarial/Registry law` with low aptitude and without wasting scarce pristine official exams on a floor-effect ritual.

## Initial assumption
The candidate is a Law graduate with rusty competitive operational knowledge until measured.

For Direito Notarial e Registral/serventias, the baseline is stronger: **no meaningful prior academic or practical exposure is assumed**. Treat the domain as genuinely new unless performance proves otherwise.

The diagnostic is for **routing**, not judgment.

## Why not begin with a mandatory 5-hour ENAC
A full official exam at zero N/R exposure can:
- produce strong floor effects in the largest block;
- reveal little about fine prerequisites;
- consume a scarce fully unseen official caderno;
- create hours of measurement before the tutor has learned how to teach the candidate;
- overweight stamina before basic reactivation.

Full simulation is valuable later; it is not automatically the best first instrument.

## Stage A — Re-entry diagnostic
Target: ~30–45 minutes.

### Sample design
Use ~24–32 items/prompts, stratified across:
- N/R foundations and general regime, interpreted primarily as **exposure/orientation baseline**;
- Civil roots with high extrajudicial transfer;
- Constitutional/Administrative delegation concepts;
- selected Tax/Business/Procedure foundations;
- one or two short free-recall prompts.

Prefer official questions or carefully grounded diagnostic items. Do not use the entire same official caderno.

### For each item capture
Before feedback:
- answer;
- confidence (coarse 0–100 or calibrated bins);
- whether the candidate knows, eliminates, or guesses;
- approximate latency only if it adds signal.

After feedback:
- error cause;
- prerequisite missing;
- whether explanation restores understanding quickly.

### Outputs
- initial legal reactivation profile;
- N/R exposure baseline;
- confidence calibration baseline;
- candidate explanation preferences observed in interaction;
- first `requires` edges justified by evidence;
- first review seeds.

Do not convert this small sample into precise percentage mastery of the entire syllabus.

## Stage B — Early calibration
After approximately 2–4 weeks of real study, or earlier if evidence shows rapid reactivation:
- 30–50 question mixed timed block;
- preserve matrix proportions approximately;
- include unseen items;
- measure accuracy, confidence, time, error recurrence and transfer.

This is the first meaningful score forecast.

## Stage C — Full simulation
Use a 100-question official or high-fidelity simulation when:
- the result can discriminate real readiness rather than only confirm floor effect;
- the candidate has enough exposure for the N/R score to represent learning rather than novelty;
- stamina/timing measurement becomes useful;
- the system has a reason to recalibrate curriculum allocation.

At least one official complete exam should remain sealed as a holdout for as long as practical.

## Holdout policy
Tag each item/exam as:
- `unseen_holdout`;
- `diagnostic_used`;
- `teaching_used`;
- `review_used`.

Never report `unseen accuracy` using an item the candidate has already encountered.

## Baseline interpretation
### N/R
Low score at zero exposure primarily measures exposure, not ceiling.

The first useful evidence is not merely whether the candidate guessed correctly, but:
- whether the architecture makes sense after explanation;
- whether the core rule can be retrieved minutes later;
- whether it transfers to a slightly changed case;
- how much survives after delay.

### General legal subjects
These better estimate degree of rust, but still separate:
- failed recall with preserved recognition;
- conceptual loss;
- terminology loss;
- reading/attention error;
- unfamiliar current-law change.

A candidate who reconstructs quickly after one explanation is different from one who lacks the concept entirely, even if both missed the initial item.

## Domain-general versus domain-specific inference
Do not convert one legal-domain error into a global cognitive label.

The diagnostic feeds:
- `architecture/LEARNER_MODEL.md` for cross-domain cognitive patterns only after repeated evidence;
- node-level Domain Performance for local knowledge/mastery.

## Adaptive consequence
The first study queue uses the diagnostic as a prior, not a permanent label. Every real study session overrides old assumptions with newer evidence.

## Success criterion
Within the first month, the system should know enough to answer:
- what foundations actually need rebuilding;
- how quickly the candidate reacquires law;
- how rapidly material decays;
- what explanation format produces transfer;
- where N/R should begin;
- when the first full simulation becomes informative.
