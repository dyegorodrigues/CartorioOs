# GX Cartório OS — Advance Organizer / Schema-First Protocol

Status: v0.1 pedagogical protocol

## Purpose
A rusty or novice learner should not be dropped into isolated legal details before seeing the architecture that gives those details meaning. Before each major learning unit, GX exposes a compressed but accurate map of the whole, then progressively zooms into parts.

This operationalizes the user's 'tree' model:

`terrain -> seed -> roots -> trunk -> branches -> leaves -> fruit`

Mapped to study:

1. **Terrain** — why the discipline exists, where it sits in the Brazilian legal/notarial system, what problems it solves.
2. **Seed** — core vocabulary and first principles.
3. **Roots** — prerequisite concepts and cross-disciplinary dependencies.
4. **Trunk** — central legal architecture: sources, institutions, principles, actors, procedures.
5. **Branches** — edital topics and subtopics.
6. **Leaves** — exceptions, deadlines, jurisprudence, special regimes, operational details.
7. **Fruit** — objective question, written reasoning, practical act and oral explanation.

## Evidence rationale
The protocol draws on convergent learning-science ideas rather than one branded method:
- advance organizers can facilitate learning and retention;
- pre-training on names and characteristics of key concepts helps learners build a model of a complex system;
- concept/knowledge maps can aid retention and transfer when used well;
- retrieval practice and spacing remain necessary because seeing a map is not mastery;
- interleaving is used selectively to discriminate similar legal categories.

The organizer is therefore a **navigation scaffold**, not a substitute for retrieval or deep legal study.

## Four zoom levels
Every major unit should expose four maps before or alongside detailed study.

### L0 — Career Map
One-page architecture of the pathway:
ENAC -> state contest -> objective (if applicable) -> written/discursive + practical -> oral -> titles -> delegation.
Also shows National Core vs State Overlay.

### L1 — Discipline Map
For each discipline:
- purpose;
- main sources;
- major families of concepts;
- connections to other disciplines;
- exam weight and phase relevance;
- what 'mastery' means in that discipline.

### L2 — Theme Map
Before a theme:
- prerequisite nodes;
- 5–15 key concepts;
- main legal questions the theme answers;
- internal structure;
- common confusions;
- where questions tend to attack;
- what changes at objective / discursive / practical / oral depth.

### L3 — Lesson Preview
Before a study session, show in 30–120 seconds:
- what will be learned;
- where it sits in the map;
- why it matters;
- what prior knowledge is being activated;
- one diagnostic/pretest prompt.

## Progressive disclosure
The learner should not face the full encyclopedia at once.

Default interface:
- **MAP**: whole architecture in compressed form;
- **MASTER**: full learnable exposition;
- **REVIEW**: high-density revision;
- **RECALL**: retrieval prompts/flashcards;
- **EXAM**: official/synthetic applications;
- **REFERENCE**: deep doctrine, historical detail, controversies and source material.

The map is always visible enough to prevent disorientation while detail is disclosed progressively.

## Flashcards policy
Flashcards are generated only for knowledge that benefits from atomic retrieval, such as:
- definitions with discriminative value;
- lists/requisites;
- competence;
- deadlines;
- exceptions;
- jurisprudential holdings;
- distinctions between similar institutes;
- procedural sequence anchors.

Avoid cards that merely fragment prose. Complex reasoning should be trained with mini-cases, free recall, written answers and oral prompts.

## Session protocol
1. **Preview map** (30–120s).
2. **Diagnostic activation**: one or a few questions before teaching.
3. **Foundation**: minimum prerequisite repair if needed.
4. **Core explanation**: legal structure and rule logic.
5. **Worked examples / concrete notarial or registral cases**.
6. **Retrieval without looking**.
7. **Official question or high-quality transfer question**.
8. **Error diagnosis**.
9. **Update map**: explicitly connect the new concept to existing nodes.
10. **Schedule spaced retrieval** based on performance, not a universal fixed interval.

## Novice/rusty-candidate rule
At baseline, assume prior legal knowledge is unreliable until demonstrated. However, do not force a complete law-school reboot. Repair prerequisites **just in time** and promote them into durable foundation nodes when they recur.

## Expertise adaptation
As mastery rises:
- maps become shorter;
- redundant explanations disappear;
- recall becomes more compressed;
- cases become less signposted;
- interleaving increases among confusable categories;
- more tasks shift to written/oral/practical production.

## Quality gate
An organizer is good only if it improves orientation without becoming another giant summary. It must be:
- accurate;
- visually scannable;
- hierarchical;
- linked to the curriculum graph;
- short relative to the material it organizes;
- updated when the curriculum/source graph changes.
